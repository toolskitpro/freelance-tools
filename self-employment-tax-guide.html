Self-Employment Tax Guide for Freelancers (2026)

US, UK and Canada - a simple explanation for beginners

If you are a freelancer, you are self-employed. That means nobody takes tax out of your payments. You must save money yourself and pay tax to the government. This guide explains the basics for the US, UK, and Canada, and shows you how to estimate your tax in seconds.

Why Freelancers Must Think About Tax

When you have a normal job, your employer removes tax from your salary every month. When you freelance, clients pay you the full amount. It feels like more money, but part of it belongs to the government. Smart freelancers save 25 to 35 percent of every payment for tax.

United States (IRS)

1. Self-Employment Tax - 15.3 percent

On top of normal income tax, US freelancers pay a self-employment tax of 15.3 percent. This covers Social Security and Medicare. Employees split this cost with their employer, but you pay both sides.

2. Income Tax

Your profit (income minus expenses) is taxed at normal rates: 10, 12, 22, or 24 percent depending on how much you earn.

3. You Must Pay Quarterly

The IRS expects estimated payments 4 times a year (April, June, September, January). If you skip them, you pay a penalty.

Common Deductions

  • Home office (part of your rent and bills)
  • Computer, software, and internet
  • Professional courses and books
  • Travel and meals for client work
  • Health insurance premiums

United Kingdom (HMRC)

1. Income Tax

You can earn 12,570 pounds tax-free (personal allowance). After that, tax is 20 percent up to 50,270 pounds, then 40 percent above that.

2. National Insurance (Class 4)

Self-employed people also pay Class 4 National Insurance: about 6 percent on profits between 12,570 and 50,270 pounds.

3. Self Assessment

Every year you fill in a Self Assessment tax return online, usually before 31 January. Keep records of all income and expenses.

Canada (CRA)

1. Federal Income Tax

The first 16,129 dollars is tax-free. Then 15 percent up to about 55,000 dollars, and 20.5 percent after that.

2. CPP Contributions

Self-employed Canadians pay both sides of the Canada Pension Plan, about 9.8 percent of your income.

3. GST/HST

Once you earn more than 30,000 dollars in a year, you must register for GST/HST and charge it to clients.

Remember: Business expenses reduce your tax. If you earn 60,000 but spent 8,000 on work (software, equipment, internet), you only pay tax on 52,000. Always keep receipts.
